Comparison: Motorola MC6800L2 vs. MC68B00

The processing cores and performance characteristics of the MC6800L2 and the MC68B00 are identical; both are rated for a maximum clock speed of 2.0 MHz. However, they represent separate eras in Motorola’s packaging technology and part-numbering conventions.

Part Number Architectural Breakdown

MC6800L2 (Early 2.0 MHz Designation)

  • MC: Motorola Integrated Circuit Prefix

  • 6800: Core 8-bit architecture reference

  • L: Ceramic Dual In-line Package (Ceramic DIP)

  • 2: Factory-binned performance tier certifying stable operation at 2.0 MHz

MC68B00 (Standardized Family Designation)

  • MC: Motorola Integrated Circuit Prefix

  • 68: Architectural family group

  • B: Integrated speed alphanumeric identifier (explicitly indicating 2.0 MHz)

  • 00: Base CPU core configuration

  • Package Suffix: Typically omitted in broad documentation, but stamped on hardware as P (Plastic) or S (Cerdip)

Hardware Interoperability Guidelines

Note

From an electrical and execution standpoint, these two microprocessors are fully interchangeable drop-in replacements. Both utilize identical 40-pin DIP layouts and require matching external two-phase non-overlapping clock driver circuitry.

Timing Equivalence Rule

Both components share identical internal bus cycle metrics:

\[f_{max} = 2.0\text{ MHz} \implies t_{cyc} \ge 500\text{ ns}\]

The minimum allowable clock pulse high width (\(PWH_{\phi\_1}\)) for both variants is strictly bounded:

\[PW_{H} \ge 180\text{ ns}\]

Warning

While a ceramic MC6800L2 can always replace a plastic MC68B00P, replacing an MC6800L2 with a plastic MC68B00P in high-temperature or high-humidity industrial settings may degrade environmental tolerance due to the shift from gold/ceramic to tin/plastic materials.
